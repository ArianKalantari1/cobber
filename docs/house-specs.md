# House specs

Service, batch, and prep specs for house drinks worked on with Cobber.
The engine suggested the flavour bridges; the amounts are bartender judgement.
None of it has been taste-tested yet unless a section says so.

---

## Olivia (venue owner's signature)

**Original:** 45 gin · 7.5 amaretto · 7.5 honey syrup · 7.5 olive oil · 10 lemon ·
foamer · basil leaf garnish.
**As made in practice:** 45 gin · 7.5 amaretto · 15 honey syrup · 20 lemon · foamer.

**What Cobber found:** amaretto with olive is the signature. It is a pairing the
classic recipes never use (tradition 0) that still shares compounds (harmony 0.18).
Whole-drink harmony was low (0.08). Of the single additions tested, French dry
vermouth raised it most (to 0.114) and orange bitters also helped (0.104).

### v2 service spec (pending owner tasting)

| Per serve | ×8 (700 ml bottle) | |
|---|---|---|
| 45 ml | 360 ml | gin washed with olive oil |
| 15 ml | 120 ml | French dry vermouth (Dolin / Noilly Prat) |
| 7.5 ml | 60 ml | amaretto |
| 12.5 ml | 100 ml | honey syrup |
| 1 dash | 7 ml | orange bitters |
| 2 drops | 1 ml | saline (20%) |
| ≈ 81 ml | ≈ 648 ml | **batch** |

**Service:** 80 ml batch + 22.5 ml fresh lemon + foamer. Shake hard (a reverse dry
shake gives a taller foam layer), double-strain into a chilled 180–220 ml coupe,
then 3 drops of basil oil on the foam. If a guest wants more olive, add 2.5–5 ml
olive oil to the shake.

If the vermouth is too noticeable, use 10 ml vermouth and 25 ml lemon. The volume
stays about the same.

**Batch storage:** about 28% alcohol, fine on the rail all shift, cool room
overnight. Vermouth fades once opened, so make bottles to last about a week.
Shake the bottle before service.

### Gin washed with olive oil
- 100 ml extra-virgin olive oil per 700 ml gin (up to about 140 ml for more olive).
- A buttery oil (e.g. Arbequina) gives a rounder drink; a peppery oil
  (Picual / Coratina) gives a more divisive peppery catch at the back of the throat.
- Shake hard, rest 4–24 h at room temperature, freeze overnight, lift off the
  solid oil, then **filter through a coffee filter** so the gin doesn't go
  cloudy in the cool room.
- Label and date it. The olive aroma fades after about 2–4 weeks.

### Basil oil garnish (shelf-stable)
Fresh basil in oil is a botulism risk at room temperature: low acid, sealed
from air, and spores live on herbs. A good green colour doesn't mean it's safe.
The safe version uses **fully dried basil**:

1. Microwave about 100 g fresh basil leaves in a single layer between paper towels,
   in 30 s bursts (1.5–3 min total), until the leaves **crumble to dust** and are
   still green. Any bendy or leathery leaf is not dry enough. Blanching first is
   optional, and the leaves must still end up completely crisp.
2. Crush the leaves (about 8–10 g dried) into 200 ml of 50/50 mild olive oil and
   grapeseed oil. Optional: 1 tsp spinach powder for colour.
3. Warm to about 60 °C, no hotter. Blend 1 min, steep 1–2 h, then strain through a
   coffee filter and let it drip.
4. Amber dropper bottle, on the rail, out of direct sun. Make 200 ml at a time.

Never use fresh leaves, water, or "lightly dried" tube basil (it still has
moisture). Worth confirming with the council's food-safety officer.

## Strawberry Alexander (Ari)

**Idea:** a twist on the Alexander, with brandy, cream liqueur, chocolate, vanilla
and strawberry. No fresh cream (shelf life) and no foamer.

**What Cobber found:** the core drink scores high harmony (0.395 with vanilla from
the Licor 43). Every one of the 16 additions tested lowered it, so don't add new
flavours: make the strawberry stronger instead.

### Strawberry and cacao brandy
- 700 ml brandy (a cheaper VS is fine, since the nibs cover the difference),
  25 g cacao nibs (optionally toasted 2 min), and 250–300 g strawberries, hulled
  and quartered (slightly overripe is cheaper and has more flavour).
- Put the nibs in a muslin or tea bag and pull them at about 24 h, before they
  turn bitter. Leave the strawberries in for 48 h, shaking daily.
- Strain through a coffee filter and **don't press the fruit**. It stays above 30%
  alcohol and keeps at room temperature. Colour and flavour soften over a few
  weeks, so store it out of light.

### Batch (no dairy)

| Per serve | ×15 | |
|---|---|---|
| 40 ml | 600 ml | brandy infused with strawberry and cacao |
| 5 ml | 75 ml | Licor 43 |
| 2 drops | 1.5 ml | saline (20%) |

**Service:** 45 ml batch + 20 ml cream liqueur (from a squeeze bottle on ice) +
10 ml Monin strawberry. Add the cream liqueur last, shake hard right away, and
strain into a chilled 150–180 ml coupe. About 95 ml.

**Garnish:** 3–4 drops of Monin strawberry on top, dragged into hearts with a
toothpick. Optional: a light dusting of cocoa on half.

**Never batch the cream liqueur with the strawberry brandy.** Over days, fruit
acid plus the higher alcohol (about 30% against the liqueur's 17%) can curdle it.
In the shaker the contact is a few minutes and the acid is small, about 2 ml of
lemon juice equivalent per serve.

**Squeeze bottle:** empty and wash it daily, never top up old cream liqueur with
new, and squeeze into the jigger so the 20 ml stays accurate.

### Optional: Monin in the batch
×12 bottle: 480 brandy · 120 Monin · 60 Licor 43 · 1.2 saline. Service: 55 ml
batch + 20 ml cream liqueur. Alcohol isn't the risk (about 30%). The risks are
**pectin clumping** (stringy bits that won't shake back in), settling, and the
colour dulling. Test a 3–4 serve bottle through 3 days of the normal rail and
cool-room routine before adopting it. If it passes, make bottles that last
3–4 days and shake before every pour.

### If the strawberry tastes weak, in this order
1. More strawberries in the infusion (300–350 g per 700 ml), which adds no sugar.
2. Swap 5 ml of Monin for crème de framboise. Raspberry had the smallest negative
   effect of the fruits tested (−0.06), and the sugar stays the same.
3. Only then add more Monin.

## Orient Espresso (espresso-martini twist; working name was Carajillo Amer)

**Idea:** a bittersweet espresso martini made only from stock already on hand.
No cream, no egg; the espresso makes the foam.

**What Cobber found:** two rounds of 10 refinement loops. Adding Mr Black was
the biggest gain (harmony 0.212 → 0.283). Averna won every bitter slot
(0.283, against 0.186 for Montenegro and 0.180 for Fernet). Brandy was the
best base (0.283; rye 0.263, bourbon 0.258, vodka 0.180).

| Per drink | ×11 (700 ml bottle) | |
|---|---|---|
| 25 ml | 275 ml | VS brandy |
| 15 ml | 165 ml | Mr Black |
| 10 ml | 110 ml | Averna |
| 10 ml | 110 ml | Licor 43 |
| 2 drops | about 1 ml | saline (20%) |

**Service:** 60 ml batch + 35 ml fresh espresso. Shake hard for 12–15 s,
fine-strain into a chilled coupe, garnish with a dehydrated orange wheel.
About 95 ml goes into the tin, giving about 119 ml in the glass at about
16.6% ABV (about 1.6 standard drinks) and about 8.5 g sugar per 100 ml. The
batch is shelf-stable.

Brandy went up to 25 and Averna down to 10 at Ari's call. The orange stays
dried, not a fresh expressed zest: in Cobber, fresh orange oil drops harmony
from 0.424 to 0.302. If the drink tastes too dry, take Licor 43 to 12.5
(about 9.1 g sugar per 100 ml). On aroma alone Averna is close to neutral
(0.424 with it, 0.444 without), so whether to keep it is a taste call (test
it at 10 ml against none).

**Sugar and ABV data used:** Mr Black 25% / 25.1 g per 100 ml (producer FAQ);
Averna 29% / 23 g per 100 ml (label data); Licor 43 31% / about 40 g per
100 ml (worked out from the label's calories, so unverified).

**Coffee-tequila (Mexican) edition, to clear stock:** if it is a dry
flavoured tequila, swap it 1:1 for the brandy. If it is a sweet liqueur, use
25 coffee tequila · 10 Mr Black · 10 Averna · 5 Licor 43 · 35 espresso ·
saline. The brand is still to be confirmed.

**More vanilla without more sugar:** 2 dashes of vanilla tincture, not more
Licor 43.

**Chocolate bitters (still to test):** Cobber has no cocoa ingredient, so I
scored a provisional proxy made of cocoa's key odorants that are in its
vocabulary (pyrazine, 2-acetylpyrrole, phenylacetaldehyde, furaneol, linalool,
vanillin, ethyl 2-methylbutyrate). The proxy is not in the data.

| Version | Harmony |
|---|---|
| Current spec | 0.424 |
| + 2 dashes cocoa bitters | 0.334 |
| Cocoa bitters in place of Averna | 0.326 |
| Mole (spiced) bitters | 0.329 |

The drop reflects the proxy's limits more than a real clash: Cobber is
missing most of the roast compounds that coffee and cocoa share. Use cocoa
bitters as an accent (2 dashes, or drops on the foam), never as a
replacement for Averna, and choose plain cocoa over mole-style.

## Summer Fling: apricot purée (simplified)

**Build:** three things go into the tin: the batch (tequila, agave, mezcal,
Floc de Gascogne and apricot liqueur), the apricot purée, and lime juice.

**Problem:** canned apricot in its full syrup tasted flat, and the apricot
didn't come through.

**Fix:** the apricot liqueur in the batch carries the apricot intensity, so
the purée stays simple: canned fruit, half the syrup, a fresh-fruit acid
blend, vitamin C and salt. No dried apricot, no cooking, no agave, no sieve
(the texture is like a frozen margarita).

**One 825 g can, makes about 600–650 ml:**

| Ingredient | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Canned apricot halves | all of the fruit (about 480 g) | body and flavour |
| Can syrup | **half** of it (about 170 g); discard the rest | less sugar, more apricot |
| Malic acid | 3 g | apricot's own main acid, which tastes like fresh fruit |
| Citric acid | 1 g | brightness (the lime does the real souring) |
| Ascorbic acid (vitamin C), optional | 1 g | colour only; skip unless a test batch browns (check the can label first: many canned fruits already list antioxidant 300) |
| Salt | 1 g | lifts the fruit |
| Potassium sorbate (optional) | 0.5 g, dissolved in a little warm water | yeast and mould control; roughly doubles fridge life |
| Monin peach purée (optional) | 30 ml | stone-fruit lactones make the apricot read riper (harmony 0.36) |

**Method:** blend everything on high for about 1 min. Canned apricots are
soft, so it blends quickly. No sieve. Use a wide-tip squeeze bottle or a pour
jug, and label and date it (note "contains preservative 202" if you use
sorbate). Never add sodium benzoate alongside the vitamin C.

**Storage:** fridge or ice well. Use within 5 days, or 10–14 days with
sorbate. Frozen portions keep 2–3 months. Out of temperature control, the
4-hour rule applies.

**Apricot liqueur in the batch:** it brings sugar as well as apricot, so for
every 10 ml of apricot liqueur per drink, take about 3 ml of agave out of the
batch.

**Service:** 30 ml purée per drink, single-strain with a Hawthorne strainer
only, and scrape the jigger.

### Summer Fling batch

Pour **60 ml** of batch per drink (a fixed 60 ml jigger stops over-pouring).
That keeps the same ratio as 35 tequila : 10 mezcal : 15 Floc : 5 apricot
liqueur : 5 agave, at about 14% less spirit cost per drink than a 70 ml pour.

| What's in each 60 ml pour | 700 ml bottle (11 pours) | |
|---|---|---|
| 30 ml | 330 ml | blanco tequila |
| 8.5 ml | 95 ml | mezcal |
| 13 ml | 140 ml | Floc de Gascogne |
| 4.25 ml | 47.5 ml | apricot liqueur |
| 4.25 ml | 47.5 ml | agave nectar, straight (95 ml if your agave is cut 1:1) |
| **60 ml** | **660 ml** | |

**Into the tin:** 60 ml batch + 30 ml apricot purée + 20 ml lime (use 17.5 ml
for a softer drink). Shake hard and short (6–8 s; the ice in the glass does
the rest of the dilution) and single-strain (Hawthorne only) over fresh
cubed ice in a rocks glass.

**The drink (estimated):** 110 ml into the tin gives about 134 ml in the glass
at about 14% ABV (about 1.5 standard drinks), with about 9.3 g sugar per
100 ml and about 1.1% acid. The batch is about 31% ABV, so it is
shelf-stable. Floc's fresh grape notes fade, so make bottles to last about
1–2 weeks and keep them in the cool room overnight.

**Why 13 ml of Floc:** at around 17% ABV it is gentle, and at 5–7 ml its
honey and stone-fruit notes disappear behind the tequila, smoke, lime and
purée. It also does double duty as part of the sweetener, which is why the
agave is so small. If it reads too strong, go to 10 ml Floc and add 2.5 ml
tequila and 1 ml agave per pour. Cobber can't score Floc (it is not in the
data), so this comes from bartending judgement and the sugar maths.

**Without apricot liqueur (test):** replace its 47.5 ml in the bottle with
tequila; the drink comes out a touch drier. If the apricot fades, add 30 ml
of Monin peach to the purée batch.

**Why these numbers:** Floc, apricot liqueur and the purée all bring sugar,
so the agave stays small. The old build (45 tequila + 10 agave + purée +
lime) worked out at about 11.8 g sugar per 100 ml; this one is drier and
more layered, with smoke from the mezcal and honey from the Floc.

**Options:** without apricot liqueur, put 30 ml of Monin peach in the purée
batch instead (about 8.8 g sugar per 100 ml, slightly drier). Tune the agave
in 2.5 ml steps: 7.5 ml gives about 10.9 g sugar per 100 ml. The sugar
figures for Floc (about 15 g/100 ml), apricot liqueur (about 28 g/100 ml) and
the purée (about 16 g/100 ml) are estimates, so check the bottle labels.

## Open tests
- Olivia v2 against the current spec, side by side (and 10 against 15 ml vermouth).
- Basil oil: microwave-dried with and without blanching; colour on the foam.
- Strawberry Alexander: Monin in the batch, 3-day stability test.
- Strawberry Alexander: 10 against 12.5 ml Monin; strawberry level in the infusion.
- Orient Espresso: blind head-to-head against the current espresso martini;
  Averna at 10 ml against none; sweetness (as specced against vanilla
  tincture against Licor 43 at 12.5); foam hold at 5 min; batch on day 1
  against day 7; service speed; a 2-week soft launch; cocoa bitters (none
  against 2 dashes in the shake against 3 drops on the foam).
