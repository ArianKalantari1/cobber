# Rescued White Wine Cobblers: Coconut & Lime / Apricot & Lemon

Status: **R&D**. Run the quarter test batch, taste on day 3, then scale up.
Last updated: 2026-10-12.

Leftover white wine from functions and by-the-glass service is cooked down with
fruit, sweetened with an oleo made from squeezed citrus husks, then fortified.
The result is one bottled **cobbler batch**. The rescued wine is the hero: each
drink carries about 64–82 ml of original wine.

---

## 1. Collecting the wine

- **Taste every bottle first.** Discard anything vinegary, corked (wet cardboard)
  or stale and sherry-like. One bad bottle ruins a whole batch.
- **Natural / no-sulphur wines spoil fastest. Use them first.** (e.g. Pazza
  Riesling, Pét Nat, Čajkov orange wine.)
- Use within **3 days**, or freeze in **500 ml tubs** in the back-of-house
  freezer.
- Label the containers with date and contents (e.g. *"Rescue White, Sancerre +
  Muscadet + Fiano, 12/10"*).
- **Oaky Chardonnay limit** (its vanilla/oak shows through):
  - Coconut: up to **40%** (oak's lactones smell like coconut, so it helps here).
  - Apricot: up to **20%**.
- Pink and rosé wines will tint the batch blush. That's fine for coconut;
  keep them out of the apricot batch.

## 2. Prep the day before

- **Oleo (replaces plain sugar):** bag the squeezed husks with the sugar and
  leave in the fridge overnight (**24 h at most**, or the pith turns bitter).
  Squeeze the bag, strain out the syrup, and keep it for cook day.
- **Collecting husks during service:** keep two zip-lock bags in the fridge,
  *"lime husks"* and *"lemon husks"*, dated.

## 3. Method (per 3 L rescued wine)

1. **Simmer** the wine with the *Start* ingredients, uncovered, until the whole
   pot (including fruit juice) is down to **1.5 L**. Mark a chopstick at the
   1.5 L level and use it as a dipstick.
2. **Off the heat:** steep any *Steep* ingredients (lid on), then stir in the
   **oleo syrup**.
3. **Fine strain, cool, fine strain again.**
4. Coconut only: chill 2 h (or 30–45 min in the back-of-house freezer), **lift
   off the set coconut fat**, and strain again.
5. **Fortify:** add the spirit, bitters and saline.
6. **Citric acid to taste.** Aim for **pH 3.3 or below** (check with a pH strip).
7. **Bottle and label:** name, date, best-by date (+6 weeks), and the pours:
   **"HH 70 / Normal 90"**.

**Result:** about **3.3 L**, roughly **18% alcohol** (assuming a 37.5% house
spirit) and about **140 g/L sugar**.
**Shelf life:** safe for 2–3 months in the fridge; **best within 6 weeks**.

## 4. Recipes

### Coconut & Lime (vodka)

| Part | Full batch (3 L wine) | Quarter test (750 ml wine) |
|---|---|---|
| Rescued white wine | 3 L → reduce to 1.5 L | 750 ml → reduce to 375 ml |
| Start | 180 g desiccated coconut, toasted golden in a dry pan · 1 can (825 g) pears in juice, juice included · 3 g salt | 45 g toasted coconut · ~200 g pears (¼ can) · 1 g salt |
| Oleo | 36 squeezed lime halves + 400 g sugar | 9 lime halves + 100 g sugar |
| Spirit | 1.4 L vodka (2 × 700 ml) | 350 ml vodka |
| Bitters | 40 ml orange bitters + 20 ml Peychaud's (start at ⅔: 27 + 13) | 10 ml orange + 5 ml Peychaud's |
| 20% saline | 4 ml | 1 ml |
| Citric | 2–4 g, to taste | ~1 g, to taste |
| Extra step | Lift off the coconut fat (step 4) | Same |

### Apricot & Lemon (gin)

| Part | Full batch (3 L wine) | Quarter test (750 ml wine) |
|---|---|---|
| Rescued white wine | 3 L → reduce to 1.5 L (rich/orange wines are ideal) | 750 ml → reduce to 375 ml |
| Start | 450 g dried apricots, chopped · 15 g fresh ginger, sliced | 115 g dried apricots · 4 g ginger |
| Steep (off the heat) | 3 chamomile teabags, 5 min, then remove | 1 chamomile teabag |
| Oleo | 36 squeezed lemon halves + 300 g sugar | 9 lemon halves + 75 g sugar |
| Spirit | 1.4 L gin (2 × 700 ml) | 350 ml gin |
| Bitters | 60 ml orange bitters (start at 40) | 15 ml (start at 10) |
| 20% saline | 4 ml | 1 ml |
| Citric | 2–4 g, to taste | ~1 g, to taste |
| Extra step | Press the apricots hard when straining | Same |

⚠️ Apricot is close to peach: get owner sign-off (Sangria Branco).

## 5. Service

### Happy hour, built (rocks glass): about 1.0 standard drink
1. 1 lemon slice + 1 lime slice in the glass, **muddle lightly** (no pith crushing).
2. **70 ml** cobbler batch.
3. Fill with cubes, top with **20–30 ml soda**, quick stir, straw.

If it tastes too sweet (there's less juice and no shake dilution), add **+2 g
citric per litre** to the batch and drop normal service to 15 ml citrus.

### Normal service, shaken (wine glass): about 1.3 standard drinks
1. **90 ml** cobbler batch + **20 ml citrus** (lime for coconut, lemon for
   apricot) + a citrus half-wheel in the tin.
2. Shake hard for 6–8 s. Dump **everything** (the ice shards stand in for crushed ice)
   into a wine glass pre-chilled with ice and soda.
3. 2–3 fresh cubes + 20 ml soda.
4. Garnish: **coconut → edible flower** (or 3–4 toasted coconut chips);
   **apricot → mint sprig**.

Busy nights (optional): crack a bucket of cubes in a tea towel before service
and keep it in an insulated ice bucket (lasts 1–2 h).

For comparison: HH light Aperol (30/90/30) is about 1.0 standard drink, and the
HH Negroni (60 ml) is about 1.25.

## 8. Sourcing dried apricots (test both, raw, side by side)

- Australian: [The Source Bulk Foods, Australian Dried Apricots](https://thesourcebulkfoods.com.au/product-list/dried-fruit/australian-apricots).
  Tart and intense; sold loose.
- [Woolworths, Angas Park Apricot Large Dried](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/47780/angas-park-apricot-large-dried).
  Check the origin on the pack.
- Skip the "Soft & Juicy Mediterranean" style (too sweet and mild).
- Bright-orange dried apricots contain sulphites (220/224). Note for allergy questions.
- Fresh apricots (about Dec–Jan): add 500 g fresh in the last 20 min, on top of
  the dried, for a fresh top note.
