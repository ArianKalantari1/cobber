# Cobbler R&D Test Sheet (quarter batches)

Test-day sheet for the two white cobblers. Full specs, costing and service are in
[rescued-white-wine-cobblers.md](rescued-white-wine-cobblers.md).
Last updated: 2026-10-21 (added the 450 ml Coconut & Lime quick test at the end).

One quarter batch = 750 ml rescued wine → about 800 ml finished batch at 16–18% alcohol.
If a quarter batch ends up over 875 ml, the pot was under-reduced: add 50 ml vodka.

---

## Day before

- [ ] Taste every wine and discard faulty bottles. 750 ml per batch. Oaky Chardonnay: up to
      40% of the coconut wine, 20% of the apricot wine. No pink wine in the apricot.
- [ ] **Citrus spirit**, 12–24 h before you fortify. Microplaned lime zest can get by on 4 h
      (zest the morning you cook). Lemon strips need the full time, so start them the night
      before:
  - Coconut: zest of **4 limes** (~12 g, green only) in **200 ml** of the quarter's vodka.
    Each 50 ml = 1 lime.
  - Apricot: peel of **3 lemons** (~24 g, Y-peeler strips, yellow only) in **150 ml** of the
    quarter's gin. Each 50 ml = 1 lemon.
  - Sealed jar, room temperature, shake 2–3 times. Frozen zest goes in still frozen. Then
    juice those limes and lemons for service.
- [ ] **Acid solution**, topped up with warm water to 100 ml (0.3 g acid per ml), dated:
  - Lime: 20 g citric + 10 g malic.
  - Lemon: 30 g citric.
- [ ] Tinctures for round 1 (start 1–3 days before). Clean glass jar rinsed with boiling
      water or vodka, lid on, room temp (18–25°C), out of the sun, shake once a day:
  - Ginger: 20 g fresh ginger, washed, unpeeled, sliced 1–2 mm, in 100 ml vodka.
    24 h; up to 72 h for stronger. Not past a week (turns woody).
  - Allspice: 5 whole berries (~1 g), lightly cracked (not powdered), in 50 ml vodka.
    24 h; no more than 48 h (turns clove-medicinal and tannic).
  - Strain through a fine strainer, then a coffee filter, into labelled, dated dropper
    bottles. Ginger is best within a month; allspice keeps 6+ months.
  - Measure doses with 1 ml and 5 ml oral syringes from a chemist (a dash is too rough).

## Cook-day steps (both batches)

1. **Simmer** the wine with the *Start* ingredients (plus 25 ml lime or lemon juice if you're
   using it), uncovered, until the whole pot is down to **375 ml**. Mark a chopstick at
   375 ml and use it as a dipstick.
2. **Off the heat:** apricot only, steep the chamomile (lid on, 5 min) and remove it. Then
   stir in the **sugar** until it's fully dissolved. **No zest in the pot.**
3. **Fine strain** (press the fruit hard), cool, fine strain again.
4. Coconut only: chill 2 h (or 30–45 min in the freezer), **lift off the set fat**, strain.
5. **Fortify with the plain spirit first:** coconut 150 ml vodka, apricot 200 ml gin. Add
   the bitters and saline.
6. **Strain the citrus spirit** (fine sieve, press the zest, then a coffee filter). Add it
   **50 ml at a time** (each 50 ml = 1 fruit), tasting cold:
   - My guess: 150 ml lime vodka (3 limes), 100 ml lemon gin (2 lemons).
   - Make up whatever you don't use with plain spirit, so the total stays 350 ml.
   - Write down the dose: × 4 = limes or lemons for the full batch.
   - **Lime check:** taste the batch neat and cold (that's how it reads in the Martinez, the
     one drink with no fresh lime), then in an HH 60 serve. The lime should be clear in both.
     Still not clear at 200 ml (4 limes)? Next time make the lime vodka stronger (6 limes in
     200 ml), not bigger.
7. **Acid** to taste: 1 ml of the solution at a time, to pH 3.3 or below (pH strip). If you
   added the juice in step 1, you may need little or none.
8. **Bottle and label** (batch, version, date). Fridge. Re-taste on day 3 and adjust only
   acid and sugar.

## Round 1: one cooked batch + tinctures

Cook coconut **A** and the apricot as specced. Split coconut A into three jars of ~265 ml:

| Jar | Add | Taste and adjust up to |
|---|---|---|
| Coconut A | Nothing (control) | |
| Coconut B | 2.5 ml ginger tincture | 5 ml |
| Coconut C | 1.5 ml allspice tincture | 2.5 ml |
| Apricot | Nothing (control) | |
| Apricot + Peychaud's | 0.5 ml Peychaud's per 100 ml | 1 ml per 100 ml |

Judge the doses cold, in the HH serve on ice: cold mutes spice, so dosing at room
temperature under-spices the drink. Add ginger 1 ml at a time and allspice 0.5 ml at a time.

Write down the dose that tastes right. If B and C win different drinks, add both tinctures
to one sample to test the combination.

- **HH 60 built:** 60 batch, 1 lemon + 1 lime slice lightly muddled, cubes, 20–30 soda.

- **Normal 100 shaken:** 100 batch + 25 citrus (lime for coconut, lemon for apricot),

**Decision rule:** if plain coconut A is close to B or C, choose A. A plain batch feeds the
most drinks, and ginger or allspice can go into single drinks as tincture drops.

## Round 2

Scale the in-pot amount by the same multiple as the winning tincture dose: for example, if
ginger won at 5 ml (double the 2.5 ml start), use 8 g in the quarter batch (30 g full batch).
This is rough, because simmering uncovered steams off aroma that a cold tincture keeps.

Cook a quarter batch of the winning version with the in-pot amounts above. Cooked ginger
tastes warmer than tincture, so confirm before scaling to the full 3 L batch.

## Coconut & Lime: 450 ml quick test (2026-10-21)

A smaller round with what was on hand: 450 ml of rescued whites, no allspice berries and no
canned pears. It's 0.6 times the quarter batch; otherwise the method is the same.

**Before anything else:** microplane the zest of 2–3 limes (about 7 g, green only) into 120 ml
of the batch's vodka and seal it. It steeps while the wine reduces and chills, about 3 hours.
The zest never goes in the pot: the simmer steams off the lime oil, and the coconut fat takes
the rest. Lime juice can go in the pot.

**Reduction**
1. Mark a chopstick at the 225 ml level in your pot: pour in 225 ml water, mark it, tip it out.
2. Toast 27 g desiccated coconut in a dry pan for 2–4 minutes, stirring, until golden. Tip it
   straight into the pot.
3. Add 450 ml white wine, the peel of a third of an orange (no pith) and that piece's juice,
   15 ml lime juice and a small pinch of salt (0.6 g).
4. Simmer gently, uncovered, down to the 225 ml mark, about 20–30 minutes. Stir now and then so
   the coconut doesn't catch.
5. Off the heat, stir in 60 g sugar until it dissolves.
6. Strain through a fine strainer, pressing the coconut hard (a cloth or nut-milk bag gets more
   out). Let it cool, then strain again.
7. Chill it 2 hours (or 30–45 minutes in the freezer), lift off the set coconut fat and strain
   once more.

**Fortify**
1. 90 ml plain vodka first.
2. **Pear liqueur, in place of the pears:** 20 ml, tasting it cold, up to 40 ml in 10 ml steps.
   For every 10 ml of liqueur, take 5 ml off the plain vodka. If it's a dry pear brandy (about
   40%, not sweet), swap it 1:1 for vodka and add 12 g more sugar to the pot.
3. Strain in the lime vodka 30 ml at a time, tasting it cold; expect about 90 ml. Make up the rest
   of the 210 ml total vodka with plain vodka.
4. Peychaud's from 2.5 ml, up to 3 ml, and 0.6 ml 20% saline (about 12 drops).
5. Acid solution (20 g citric and 10 g malic, topped up to 100 ml with warm water), 1 ml at a time
   until a pH strip reads 3.3 or lower. Expect about 2 ml.
6. If it tastes lean, dissolve 5 g more sugar in a splash of warm batch and stir it back in.

Makes about 480 ml at 16–18% alcohol. Bottle, label and refrigerate; taste again on day 3.

**Spice stand-ins (tinctures for the split jars)**
- **Clove-cinnamon, for allspice:** 2 whole cloves and a 3 cm piece of cinnamon stick (about 1 g)
  in 50 ml vodka. Taste at 12 hours and strain by 24 hours at most; clove turns medicinal fast.
- **Ginger:** 20 g fresh ginger, sliced, in 100 ml vodka for 24 hours.
- **No juniper:** it's piney and pulls the batch towards gin, which is the apricot batch's job.

| Jar (about 160 ml each) | Add | Taste up to |
|---|---|---|
| A, plain | Nothing | |
| B, ginger | 1.5 ml ginger tincture | 3 ml |
| C, clove-cinnamon | 0.5 ml, then 0.25 ml at a time | 1.2 ml |

Use a 1 ml syringe. To make one spiced batch instead of testing, simmer 1 clove and a 2 cm piece
of cinnamon stick with the wine and remove them when you strain. No more than that, or it turns
into mulled wine.
