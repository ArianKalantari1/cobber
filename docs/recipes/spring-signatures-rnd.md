# Spring Signatures R&D (test sheet + service plan)

New signatures for the spring/summer menu, one per base: rum (Watermelon Reviver), pisco
(La Pasión) and tequila (C'est Chamoy). Also the reinvented Olivia, which is already on the menu.
Each drink has its current spec, batch and service, then tests, tasting notes and why.
Last updated: 2026-10-21.

House rules these follow:
- Every prep keeps about a month (freezing half is fine).
- Service is batch plus at most 2 fresh items.
- All batches are for 700 ml rail bottles.
- No herby or medicinal flavours, nothing Thai, no saffron.
- No owner overlaps: vanilla (already in the Pop Star, Apple Pie Spritz and Sangria Branco),
  pineapple, peach, apple, honey, elderflower, Chartreuse, egg-white sours, Prosecco spritzes,
  Old Fashioned formats, lychee martinis. Passionfruit is approved for La Pasión only.
- No frozen drinks, no crushed ice, no big cubes. The freezer is back of house only.
- No per-drink peeling, xanthan, grated garnishes or pectic enzyme.
- Nuts never go into a shared tin (see [Allergens](#allergens-bar-wide)).

## Shared preps

- **Sorbate stock:** dissolve 10 g potassium sorbate in about 80 ml water, then top up to the
  100 ml mark. 1 ml holds 0.1 g. Keep it in the fridge.
- **20% saline.**
- **Squeeze bottles:** one per prep, labelled and dated. Cut the tip to about 5 mm (you can cut
  more, never less) and check the cap still seals. Clean with a bottle brush and hot water between
  batches.

## 1. Watermelon Reviver (white rum), coupe

### Freeze-concentrated H2melon

1. Pour about 10% (about 125 ml) off a 1.25 L bottle; it makes room for the ice and is your test
   juice. Use the original bottle, never glass.
2. Freeze it capped for a full 24 hours, until it's rock hard.
3. Thaw it upside down in the cool room: cap off, neck down in a jug, cling film around the neck.
   (Upright also works if you pour the liquid off every 1–2 hours.)
4. Stop at about half, 500–550 ml, or when the drip runs pale. Throw the ice away.
5. Make the cordial straight away.

It comes out about 1.6 times as strong as the juice.

**About H2melon:** watermelon juice with cane juice, not from concentrate, 9.5 g sugar and 40 mg
sodium per 100 ml. Use it within 3 days of opening, so freeze it the day you open it. Sold at
Dan Murphy's, IGA and Chemist Warehouse (1.25 L bottles and 500 ml cans).

### Rose watermelon cordial (about 560 ml, 18 drinks)

Per 500 ml concentrate:
- 60 ml Monin Rose
- 4.5 g malic and 4.5 g citric
- 2 ml sorbate stock
- Check with a pH strip that it's 3.5 or lower.

Keeps 4–6 weeks in the fridge; freeze the extra.

### Per drink

| Ingredient | B (main test) | A (comparison) |
|---|---|---|
| White rum | 25 ml | 25 ml |
| Lillet Rosé | 20 ml | 20 ml |
| Orange curaçao | 10 ml | 15 ml |
| Rose watermelon cordial | 30 ml | 30 ml |
| Lemon | 15 ml | 10 ml |
| Absinthe (optional) | 1 dash | 1 dash |
| 20% saline | 0–3 drops, only if it tastes flat | same |

Shake hard for about 10 seconds with good, dry ice and double-strain into a chilled coupe. About
100 ml before shaking, about 125 ml in the glass, about 1.5 standard drinks.

### Batch (700 ml, 8 drinks)

| Ingredient | Bottle |
|---|---|
| White rum | 200 ml |
| Lillet Rosé | 160 ml |
| Orange curaçao | 80 ml |
| Rose watermelon cordial | 240 ml |
| Absinthe | 7 ml |
| 20% saline | none at first; up to 1.2 ml if it tastes flat |

H2melon is already salty (concentrated, each 30 ml of cordial carries about 4 drops' worth of
saline), so taste before adding saline.

**Service:** tip the bottle gently to mix, pour 85 ml batch and 15 ml lemon, shake hard for about
10 seconds and double-strain into a chilled coupe.

**Keeping:** in the fridge (Lillet and watermelon), 3–4 weeks. The watermelon aroma fades first,
so make one bottle at a time.

**Cucumber, for a fresher smell (optional):** heat-treatment strips watermelon's fresh, green
notes, and cucumber shares them.
- 5-minute pre-test: three glasses of cold H2melon: plain; with one thin cucumber slice muddled
  in for 5 minutes, then strained; and the same plus a pinch of salt. If plain doesn't taste
  cooked, skip the cucumber.
- Small trial: 3 thin slices (about 15 g) in 200 ml batch, fridge overnight, taste against plain
  batch, strain.
- Full bottle: about 50 g (a third of a Lebanese cucumber), 12–24 hours in the fridge, then strain
  it out. Never leave it in the bottle: it turns bitter, varies drink to drink and blocks the pour.

**If you use fresh watermelon instead:** buy whole and seedless, scrub the rind before cutting,
use clean gear, cut away the green and white layers, and cut, blend, strain, bottle and freeze
within about an hour (NSW allows cut fruit 2 hours out of the fridge in total). Thaw only in the
cool room. The raw cordial keeps about 2 weeks.

### Tasting notes

| Date | Version | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-20 | Watermelon juice | Too thin. A little rose syrup helped the texture; rose, absinthe and watermelon went well together |
| 2026-10-21 | 30 rum, 20 Lillet Rosé, 20 curaçao, 30 juice, 10 lemon, 5 rose (115 ml) | Overflowed the coupe, and flat. Trimmed to the spec above, with the rose moved into the cordial |

### Why
- **Rum:** the only rum on the menu is the Old Fashioned option, and none of the 11 watermelon
  drinks in the Sydney dataset use rum. Hawksmoor's Double Melon Daiquiri shows the pairing works.
- **Concentrate:** 30 ml of cordial carries the watermelon of about 43 ml of juice, with nearly
  twice the body, so it fixes the thinness without overfilling the coupe.
- **Malic and citric 1:1:** watermelon's own acids are mainly malic and citric; more malic tastes
  rounder and less like a lemon drink.
- **Acid in the cordial:** brings the drink close to the classic Reviver's brightness without
  adding volume.

## 2. La Pasión (pisco), highball

Passionfruit replaced guava in the pisco slot on 2026-10-21 because it's much easier to batch.
The macadamia was dropped the same day because of allergen cross-contact in shared tins. The
current direction is nut-free: fino (in stock) with burnt-sugar hibiscus syrup, against a sweet
fortified wine from the Summer Hill Wine Shop. No clarification.

### Passionfruit base (one 840 g can, about 1.1 L, 16 drinks)

1. Shake the pulp with 280 ml Sauvignon Blanc in rounds, without ice, with the Hawthorne spring
   in the tin. This loosens the pulp without cracking the seeds (a blender cracks them).
2. Fine-strain it, pressing with a bar spoon.
3. Stir in 160 ml hibiscus syrup (plain or burnt-sugar) and 4 ml sorbate stock.
4. Put it in squeeze bottles. It keeps 2–3 weeks in the fridge; freeze half.

- **No citric:** the pulp is sour enough. If the drink is sour, the fix is sugar: 200 ml syrup per
  can (12.5 ml per drink).
- **With a sweet fortified wine in the batch:** cut the syrup to about 120 ml per can.
- **Cans:** Woolworths 170 g for tests (20.1 g sugar per 100 g, against John West's 21.4 g, so the
  same recipe; one can makes 3 test drinks). John West 840 g for service, in food-service cases
  of 12.
- **Purée version** (Adamance 1 kg tub, unsweetened, seedless): 280 g sugar, 665 ml Sauvignon
  Blanc, 380 ml hibiscus syrup and 7 ml sorbate stock. Pour about 57 ml. Re-check the sugar once
  the canned version is settled.

### Hibiscus syrup (rich, about 560 ml)

1. Steep 10 g dried hibiscus in 250 ml just-boiled water for 5 minutes only, then strain and press.
   A longer steep adds harsh, drying tannins.
2. While it's warm, stir in 500 g sugar.
3. Cool it, then add 1 ml sorbate stock. Keeps a month or more in the fridge.

Gentler option: cold-steep in the fridge for 8–12 hours and warm it no hotter than 60 °C to
dissolve the sugar.

### Burnt-sugar hibiscus syrup (caramel version, swaps 1:1 for the plain one)

1. Steep 15 g dried hibiscus in 300 ml just-boiled water for 5 minutes, strain, keep it hot.
   (More hibiscus to hold the red against the caramel; more water because some boils off.)
2. In a deep pot, boil 500 g white sugar with 100 ml water. Swirl, don't stir, until it's medium
   amber, like maple syrup. Lighter is sweeter and pinker; darker is more bitter and browner.
3. Off the heat, whisk in the hot tea a little at a time. Stand back: it spits.
4. Back on low heat, stir until any hard caramel dissolves.
5. Cool it, add 1 ml sorbate stock, and top up with water to 560 ml if it's short.

It has the same sugar as the plain syrup, so the drink doesn't get sweeter; if anything it's a
little less sweet. Expect rosy-amber rather than bright pink. No-burn backup: make the plain
recipe with demerara (the bar has it for the Nutty Old Fashioned).

### Pisco batches (700 ml, about 15 drinks at 45 ml)

| Version | Pisco | Accent | Angostura | 20% saline | Standard drinks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain | 684 ml | none | 12 ml | 4 ml | 1.6 | Control |
| Fino (current pick) | 475 ml | fino 200 ml | 12 ml | 4 ml | 1.3 | With burnt-sugar syrup. Fridge, use within 2–3 weeks, batch from a fresh bottle |
| Manzanilla | 475 ml | manzanilla 200 ml | 12 ml | 2 ml | 1.3 | Saltier than fino, so half the saline |
| Dry oloroso, amontillado or medium | 475 ml | 200 ml | 12 ml | 4 ml | 1.3 | With burnt-sugar syrup. Medium is somewhat sweet: if it tastes sweet, cut the base syrup toward 120 ml per can |
| Sweet: Topaque, tawny, cream sherry, Madeira | 525 ml | 150 ml | 12 ml | 4 ml | 1.4–1.5 | Cut the base syrup to about 120 ml per can; burnt sugar optional |

**Peychaud's instead of Angostura** (same 12 ml) pulls the colour back to pink if the caramel
browns it, with a light cherry-anise note.

### Service

1. Shake the base bottle. Pour 70 ml base and 45 ml pisco batch into the tin.
2. Shake hard with cubes for 5–8 seconds, then dump everything into a highball. This mixes the
   thick passionfruit through the drink, so guests don't have to dig it up.
3. Fill with fresh cubes and top with about 40 ml soda water.
4. Black straw.

### Next test, side by side

Make a little base without syrup. Each drink gets 60 ml of it plus 10 ml syrup:

| # | Syrup | Spirit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plain hibiscus | 45 ml plain pisco batch (control) |
| 2 | Burnt-sugar hibiscus | 30 ml plain pisco batch + 15 ml fino |
| 3 | Burnt-sugar hibiscus | 40 ml plain pisco batch + 5 ml Averna (caramel and cola, but it can read herbal) |
| 4 | Plain hibiscus | 35 ml plain pisco batch + 10 ml Topaque (after the wine shop) |
| 5 | Burnt-sugar hibiscus | 30 ml plain pisco batch + 15 ml Elegante Medium sherry (after the wine shop) |

Then try these one at a time on the winner:
- Peychaud's instead of Angostura, for colour.
- 5 ml Cointreau: orange lifts both passionfruit and sherry.
- Half soda, half lemonade on top: fino with lemon-lime soda is Spain's Rebujito, and it adds
  sweetness without more syrup.

### What to buy (Summer Hill Wine Shop)

See [suppliers.md](suppliers.md) for the shop's details and full fortified range.
1. **Buller Fine Old Topaque, $26** (pick). At least 10 years old, butterscotch and toffee, and it
   keeps for months once open. Morris Classic Topaque ($25) is a fine swap.
2. **Gonzalez Byass Elegante Medium sherry, $25 ($19.99 in a six)** (sherry pick). The shop has
   no amontillado or dry oloroso (the Osborne Bailén is gone), and this is the closest: Palomino
   and Pedro Ximénez, with nuts, wood and cocoa and a toasted-caramel finish. No vanilla in its
   notes. It sits between the fino and the Topaque, nutty like one and caramel like the other, so
   it suits the burnt-sugar syrup. Somewhat sweet: use the dry-sherry batch row and cut the syrup
   if it tastes sweet.
3. **Valdespino Inocente Fino 375 ml, $32 ($24 in a six).** If the bar's fino tastes thin in
   test 2. Over 8 years under flor, so it's fuller than most finos and still dry. Tío Pepe ($40)
   is the standard fino; La Guita manzanilla ($19.99) is the salty one.
4. **Little Giant Fine Aged Tawny 375 ml, $19.99.** The cheapest test bottle. Dow's Fine Tawny
   ($38) is the step up.

Skip Pedro Ximénez (too sweet and dark), Solera 1847 Cream and Leonor Palo Cortado (vanilla in
their notes), ruby or LBV port (berry, not caramel), and anything labelled vanilla or salted
caramel. At 10–13 ml a drink, any bottle under about $55 costs under a
dollar a drink.

### Parked ideas

- **Cacao nib vodka** (no vanilla, no sugar): toast 30 g nibs in a dry pan for 5 minutes if
  they're raw, add 200 ml vodka, taste daily and strain through a coffee filter at 2–3 days. Use
  it in a spray for a cocoa nose, or swap 50 ml into the batch. Passionfruit and chocolate is a
  pastry classic (Pierre Hermé's Mogador macaron). Buy nibs without a "may contain nuts" warning.
- **White crème de cacao:** dropped because of its vanilla note and extra sugar.
- **Wattleseed** (Australian native, roasted hazelnut, cocoa and coffee; not a nut, but a legume,
  so name it on the menu): 20 g ground roasted wattleseed per 700 ml batch, taste at 4, 12 and
  24 hours, strain through a coffee filter. Version 2, when there's time.
- **Lúcuma** (Peruvian; caramel, maple and sweet potato, brings back creaminess): blend 30 g powder
  into each can's base before fine-straining. The colour drifts to coral.
- **Purple corn** (Peru's chicha morada colour): simmer 100 g dried purple corn in 500 ml water for
  about 45 minutes, down to about 250 ml, and use it instead of the hibiscus tea.
- **Pomegranate instead of hibiscus:** make the syrup with 250 ml 100% pomegranate juice (not an
  apple blend) instead of the tea; warm it just enough to dissolve the sugar.
- **Sichuan pepper:** 2 g in the cacao nib vodka for the spray. Divisive; test on staff first.

**Ruled out:** ginger ale (El Diablito), toasted rice or popcorn (the Pop Star), coconut (the
cobbler), pink peppercorn (cashew family), chinotto (divisive), tonic (takes over), grapefruit
soda (grapefruit-forward), Aperol (not needed), crushed ice, clarification.

### If macadamia ever comes back: the allergy-safe build

- **Macadamia vodka:** 400 g roasted salted macadamias in 700 ml house vodka at room temperature
  for up to a week. Strain, freeze, skim the fat, then coffee-filter. Dilute with plain vodka to
  taste.
- **Keep the nut out of the tin:** pisco batch of 680 ml pisco and 18 ml Angostura (23 drinks at
  30 ml). Shake 70 ml base with 30 ml of it, dump, add fresh cubes, then pour 15 ml macadamia
  vodka over the ice from a 15 ml measured pourer, then soda. No bar spoon after the nut goes in,
  and no macadamia spray (the mist drifts onto the bar).
- **The bottle:** label it "CONTAINS MACADAMIA", give it a different-coloured pourer and keep it
  off the vodka rail; it looks exactly like plain vodka. Skimming and filtering take out fat, not
  necessarily nut protein.

### Tasting notes

| Date | Version | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-21 | Passionfruit against guava | Passionfruit wins on ease of batching. Macadamia orgeat separated (its fat). Needs more sugar. Tonic top tasted "more like tonic", grapefruit soda was grapefruit-forward, chinotto was bittersweet and divisive |
| 2026-10-21 | Canned pulp shaken with Sauvignon Blanc | Very passionfruity and a bit sour, with no citric added. The fix is sugar, hence the hibiscus syrup |
| 2026-10-21 | Macadamia in the tin | Allergen trace risk in shared tins; macadamia dropped |

### Why
- **Sauvignon Blanc:** it shares passionfruit's tropical sulphur aromas (3-sulfanylhexanol and its
  acetate), so it amplifies the passionfruit, and it costs $15 a bottle against pisco's $50.
- **Hibiscus:** pink colour. As a rich syrup, sugar leads and the tartness stays in the background.
- **Fino and sherry:** salty and nutty like the salted macadamia, with no nuts and no sugar. Sherry,
  pisco and Sauvignon Blanc are all grape. Sherry highballs are proven (the Rebujito), and real
  bars pair sherry with passionfruit: The Sun Tavern in London (amontillado, passionfruit, soda)
  and "Walk This Way" on Kindred Cocktails (cream sherry and passionfruit). Cobber's pairing score
  for passionfruit and sherry is 0.42, the same as for pisco and passionfruit.
- **No vanilla:** it's already in three menu drinks.

## 3. C'est Chamoy (tequila and mezcal), rocks glass with a Tajín rim

Say it "say chah-MOY". For the menu:

> **C'est Chamoy** 24
> Tequila, Mezcal, Floc de Gascogne, Apricot Chamoy, Lime, Tajín
> *Sweet, Salty, Spicy*

Staff line: "Chamoy started out as a salted dried apricot from China, and ours is made in house
from apricots."

### Batch (700 ml, 11 drinks at 60 ml; keeps for months)

| Ingredient | Bottle |
|---|---|
| Tequila | 440 ml |
| Mezcal | 110 ml |
| Floc de Gascogne, blanc | 110 ml |
| Fire bitters | 9 ml, only if it needs more heat |

No saline: Tajín is about half salt. The chamoy stays out of the batch, because fruit settles.

### Apricot chamoy (one Woolworths 825 g can of apricot halves in syrup, about 1 L, 34 drinks)

1. Blend the apricots with all the syrup, 200 ml Monin Agave, 20 g Tajín, 10 g citric and 20 ml
   white wine vinegar.
2. Simmer for 2 minutes. Off the heat, stir in 4 ml sorbate stock.
3. Cool it covered, bottle it, and label it with the date.
4. Keep half in the fridge and freeze the other half.

- Taste before bottling: adjust the agave first, then the Tajín. If the drink tastes salty with
  the rim, use 15 g Tajín next time.
- White wine vinegar and white Floc keep it orange; red vinegar or Floc rouge tint it pink.
- No fine-straining needed. If skins leave bits, push it once through a normal sieve.
- **Goulburn Valley apricots in juice (700 g jar)** are less sweet: 200 ml agave, 15 g Tajín, 8 g
  citric, 15 ml white wine vinegar, 3.5 ml sorbate stock. About 870 ml, 29 drinks.

| Chamoy method | Fridge | Frozen |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked and with sorbate | About 4 weeks | Months |
| Only one of the two | About 2 weeks | Months |

### Service

1. Shake the chamoy bottle.
2. Pour 60 ml batch, 30 ml chamoy and 15 ml lime into the tin.
3. Shake and dump everything into the glass; no straining.
4. Tajín on the **outside** of the rim only.

### Tests
- Fire bitters: none against 1 dash. Tajín gives warmth, not real heat.
- Tajín level in the chamoy, tasted with the rim.

### Why
- **Apricot:** wide open on the menu; only the Charlie Chaplin uses it.
- **Homemade chamoy:** bottled chamoy is mostly chilli, salt and red colouring, often thickened
  with xanthan. Ours makes the apricot the star, and Tajín covers the chilli, lime and salt.
- **Orange, not pink:** the Reviver and La Pasión are already pink.
- **Floc de Gascogne:** the French link for the name.

## 4. Olivia, reinvented (coupe)

Batched so anyone can make it in three pours, with basil dots instead of olive-oil drops.

### Olive-oil gin

1. Combine 700 ml gin and 120 ml extra virgin olive oil in a wide-mouth jar (oil sets in a bottle's
   neck) and shake well.
2. Leave it 4–24 hours at room temperature, shaking now and then.
3. Freeze it overnight, lift off the set oil, then pour the gin through a coffee filter.

Keeps for months; the oil goes to the kitchen. For basil in the gin as well, use 30 ml basil oil
and 90 ml olive oil.

### Batch (700 ml, 9 drinks)

| Ingredient | Per drink | Bottle |
|---|---|---|
| Olive-oil gin | 45 ml | 405 ml |
| Amaretto | 7.5 ml | 67.5 ml |
| Honey syrup (1:1) | 15 ml | 135 ml |
| Water | 10 ml | 90 ml |
| 20% saline | 2 drops | 1 ml |

**Service:** 75 ml batch, 12.5 ml lemon and the foamer. Shake with ice, strain out the ice, shake
again without ice, fine-strain into the coupe and let it stand 30 seconds. Finish with 3 dots of
basil tincture (drag a toothpick through them for hearts).

- Still sharp: lemon to 10 ml.
- Now too sweet: back to 12.5 ml honey syrup and 12.5 ml water.

### Basil tincture for the dots

- **Dried-basil test first (Ari's plan):** crush about 1 g microwave-dried basil into 25 ml vodka,
  steep for an hour and pour through a coffee filter. Check a drop on a white plate: expect
  olive-green rather than bright green. No blanching, no waste, keeps longer.
- **For bright green:** blanch 10 g fresh basil leaves for 5–10 seconds, plunge into ice water and
  squeeze very dry. Blend with 50 ml vodka for a minute, rest 10 minutes, then strain through a
  fine strainer and a coffee filter into an amber dropper bottle. Fridge; greenest for 2–3 weeks.

### Tasting notes

| Date | Version | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-21 | Basil oil drops on the foam | Didn't float: oil breaks egg-white foam. Switched to an alcohol tincture |
| 2026-10-21 | 15 ml lemon, 10 ml 1:1 honey syrup | Too sour. Rebalanced to the spec above |

### Why
- **Less lemon, more honey:** the original's 7.5 ml of oil in the glass rounded off the lemon. The
  fat-wash takes the oil out, and the olive oil's bitterness in the gin makes sourness harsher.
- **Saline:** rounds off both the lemon and the bitterness, and suits the savoury note.

## Allergens (bar-wide)

- **Nothing nutty goes into a shared tin, jigger, spoon or strainer.** Nut ingredients go straight
  into the glass, or the drink is nut-free. Rinsing doesn't reliably remove nut protein.
- **For a declared allergy:** tin, jigger, strainer and spoon straight from the glasswasher, never
  rinse-only gear. Wash hands, and make the allergy drink first in the round.
- **Say the nut by name** ("macadamia", "almond"), not just "nuts", and never promise nut-free:
  "made with clean gear, in a bar that uses nuts", and let the guest decide.
- **Nut infusions:** label "CONTAINS <NUT>", use a different-coloured pourer, keep them off the
  main rail. Skimmed or filtered still counts as containing nut.
- **Check which products actually contain nuts:** the orgeat (Espresso Sour), the amaretto (Olivia,
  sours), and the Skrewball and walnut (Nutty Old Fashioned), so staff can answer guests.
- **The menu marks egg but not nuts:** add a nut mark.
- **New products:** buy packs with no "may contain nuts" warning.
- **Wattleseed** is a legume (acacia): name it on the menu if it's used.
- **Staff training:** "All about Allergens", free online food-service training from the
  [National Allergy Council](https://nationalallergycouncil.org.au/programs/food-service/food-allergen-management-training).
