# Pisco and sherry: five more drinks

Other drinks on La Pasión's frame (pisco the hero, sherry for depth), with a different fruit
each time. La Pasión holds the spring pisco slot, so these are swaps if it doesn't win, or for
later seasons. None has been tasted.
Last updated: 2026-10-21.

They follow the house rules in [spring-signatures-rnd.md](spring-signatures-rnd.md): no fruit the
menu already owns (peach, apple, raspberry, cassis, grapefruit, pineapple, apricot, passionfruit,
cherry, and the cobblers' pear, plum and pomegranate), no egg white, no Old Fashioned format,
service is a batch plus at most 2 fresh items, and preps keep about a month (freezing half is
fine). Each one keeps pisco at 40 ml and the sherry at 15–20 ml. The numbers come from the same
maths as La Pasión's loop; the acid in Davidson plum and verjuice is an estimate, so balance those
by taste.

## 1. Fresas con Crema (strawberries and cream)

The most approachable of the five, and pink with no colouring. The cream sherry is the "cream".

- **Per drink:** 40 ml pisco, 20 ml Elegante Cream, 30 ml strawberry cordial, 20 ml lemon. Shake
  5–8 seconds, dump into a highball, fresh cubes, top with 70 ml soda.
- **Batch:** pisco and Elegante Cream 2:1, 60 ml a drink.
- **Strawberry cordial:** 1 kg frozen strawberries and 500 g white sugar, covered in the fridge for
  24 hours (thawing draws the juice out), then strained without pressing hard. No cooking, so it
  tastes fresh, not jammy. About 1.1 L; 2 weeks in the fridge, freeze half.
- **Numbers:** about 200 ml, 9.6% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks, sugar-to-acid about 12:1.
- **Why:** Cobber gives strawberry and sherry real shared chemistry (0.29) but only one bar drink
  (Pretty In Pink), so it's novel; strawberry and lemon is classic (10 drinks).

## 2. Ciruela (Davidson plum)

The most novel, and the native one. Davidson plum is intensely sour and deep magenta, so the
same fruit gives colour and tartness. Its dark-plum note suits the Medium's oxidised, dried-fruit
side.

- **Per drink:** 40 ml pisco, 15 ml Elegante Medium, 25 ml Davidson plum cordial, 15 ml lemon.
  Shake, dump, fresh cubes, top with 80 ml soda. Taste before adding the lemon: the plum may
  carry enough acid on its own.
- **Batch:** pisco and Elegante Medium, 40:15 (55 ml a drink).
- **Davidson plum cordial:** 500 g frozen Davidson plums, 500 g sugar and 250 ml water, warmed
  until the fruit collapses (about 10 minutes), blended briefly and fine-strained. About 900 ml;
  add 2 ml sorbate stock and it keeps a month.
- **Numbers:** about 195 ml, 9.6% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks, about 11:1.
- **Why:** Cobber scores Davidson plum at 0.30 novelty with both pisco and sherry and finds no bar
  drinks with either: the least-trodden pairing here. Its profile is provisional.
- **Buying:** Bush to Bowl, Terrey Hills, sells frozen 500 g packs for pickup. The fruit is in
  season November to February, so stock up then; freeze-dried powder is the backup.

## 3. Tres Uvas (three grapes)

The simplest service on the menu: one pour and soda. Pisco is grape brandy, manzanilla is grape
wine and verjuice is unripe grape juice, so the whole drink is grape. Best with an aromatic pisco.

- **Per drink:** 107.5 ml batch over cubes in a highball, top with 75 ml soda, one lift with a bar
  spoon. A frozen white grape on a pick if you want a garnish.
- **Batch (6 drinks, 645 ml):** 240 ml pisco, 90 ml La Guita Manzanilla, 270 ml Maggie Beer verjuice,
  45 ml simple syrup (1:1). About 17% ABV. The manzanilla fades first, so use it within 2 weeks.
- **Numbers:** about 190 ml, 9.5% ABV, 1.4 standard drinks, about 10:1. Dry and crisp, like a
  wine spritz. If it's too sharp, add simple 2.5 ml a drink at a time.
- **Why:** Cobber can't score this one: its pisco, sherry and grape profiles are placeholders.
- **Buying:** Maggie Beer Verjuice at Woolworths, about 10.5 g sugar per 100 g. It contains
  sulphites, as sherry does.

## 4. Mango con Sal

Summer's crowd-pleaser: mango with the manzanilla's salt. Mango is tropical, like La Pasión, so
pick one or the other for a menu, not both.

- **Per drink:** 40 ml pisco, 15 ml La Guita Manzanilla, 50 g Kesar mango pulp, 15 ml lime. Shake, dump,
  fresh cubes, top with 60 ml soda. No syrup: the pulp is already sweetened.
- **Batch:** pisco and La Guita, 40:15 (55 ml a drink). Optional: 3 ml 20% saline per 700 ml if
  the manzanilla's salt doesn't come through.
- **Mango:** canned Kesar mango pulp from an Indian grocer (850 g; Kashish or KTC have 20–23 g
  sugar per 100 g and citric acid). Once opened, about 5 days in the fridge; freeze it in
  portions. No chilli: Tajín is C'est Chamoy's.
- **Numbers:** about 205 ml, 8.9% ABV, 1.4 standard drinks, about 10:1.
- **Why:** mango and lime is well trodden (10 bar drinks in Cobber); sherry and mango has one
  (Jardin).

## 5. Sidecar de Jerez

A Sidecar with the base split between pisco and the Medium: orange and nut, no egg. Everything
but the lemon is shelf-stable, so the batch keeps for months.

- **Per drink:** 77.5 ml batch and 20 ml lemon, shaken hard, fine-strained into a coupe.
- **Batch (9 drinks, 700 ml):** 360 ml pisco, 135 ml Elegante Medium, 135 ml Cointreau, 70 ml
  simple syrup (1:1).
- **Numbers:** about 120 ml, 20% ABV, 1.9 standard drinks, about 7:1 (Sidecar-tart).
- **Why:** sherry and orange liqueur is a classic pair in Cobber (0.38: the Up to Date and the
  Chaplin), and pisco and lemon has 48 bar drinks.
- **Overlap:** Cointreau and lemon are also in the Jasmine; the Classics already have a pisco sour
  (with egg).

## Bonus: Pisco Bamboo (Martini Corner)

No fruit, but it fits the Martini Corner, where every drink already uses manzanilla: 30 ml pisco,
30 ml La Guita Manzanilla, 15 ml dry vermouth, 2 dashes orange bitters, stirred. Batch with 20 ml water a
serve and keep it in the fridge. Sherry and dry vermouth is Cobber's strongest sherry pairing
(0.48, the Bamboo).

## Strawberry and mango, more sophisticated

Two drinks per fruit: a long signature and a stirred one for the Martini Corner. Each uses at
most 3 fresh ingredients when batching (here 1–2), and service stays a batch plus at most 2
fresh items. Fresa y Jerez and Mango Asado share one rail batch: pisco and Elegante Medium,
40:15, 55 ml a drink (480 ml pisco and 180 ml Medium per 700 ml bottle, 12 drinks).

| Drink | Format | Fresh at batching | The idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresa y Jerez | Highball | Strawberries | Sherry two ways: the wine in the batch, the vinegar in a shrub (refined below) |
| Fresa Martini | Stirred | Strawberries | Strawberry pisco and manzanilla; native strawberry gum repeats strawberry's own aroma |
| Mango Asado | Highball | Mangoes, limes | Charred mango, so the caramel meets the Medium's toasted caramel |
| Mango y Manzanilla | Stirred | Mangoes | Mango pisco, manzanilla salt and a dash of spice |

### Fresa y Jerez (strawberry and sherry)

Refined into a one-day prep with Fresa Caramelo: see
[Strawberry highballs: one prep day](#strawberry-highballs-one-prep-day).

### Fresa Martini

- **Per serve:** 97.5 ml from the fridge, poured into a chilled glass. No stirring at service.
- **Batch (7 serves, 700 ml rail):** 315 ml strawberry pisco, 140 ml La Guita Manzanilla, 70 ml dry
  vermouth, 17.5 ml simple syrup, 140 ml water. About 22% ABV, 1.7 standard drinks, dry (about 2 g
  sugar per 100 ml). Use within 2 weeks: the manzanilla fades first.
- **Strawberry pisco:** 300 g sliced strawberries in 700 ml pisco, fridge, 48 hours, then strain
  without pressing (pressing clouds it). Pale pink; it drifts towards salmon over weeks.
- **Optional native accent:** 1 g ground dried strawberry gum leaf in the strawberry pisco for
  its last 6–12 hours; taste at 6. Strawberry gum's oil is mostly methyl cinnamate, the
  fruity-balsamic note in strawberries themselves (Cobber harmony 0.50), so it reads as more
  strawberry, not eucalyptus. If it tastes minty or medicinal, it's out (house rule). Warndu sells
  whole dried leaf all year; FISH sells 30 g ground for $15.

### Mango Asado (charred mango)

- **Per drink:** 55 ml pisco–Medium batch, 50 ml charred mango cordial, 15 ml lime. Shake, dump,
  fresh cubes, top with 60 ml soda.
- **Charred mango cordial (about 1.45 L, 29 drinks):** cheek 4 ripe mangoes (about 1 kg flesh).
  Char them flesh-down in a dry, very hot pan until blackened in patches (3–4 minutes), then scoop
  the flesh. Blend with 400 g sugar, 100 ml lime juice, 300 ml water and 2 g salt, fine-strain, add
  2 ml sorbate stock. About a month in the fridge; freeze half.
- **Off season:** 850 g canned Kesar pulp with burnt sugar instead of the char (the method of
  La Pasión's old burnt-sugar syrup: take the sugar to medium amber, then whisk in the lime and
  water before blending in the pulp). Cut the sugar to about 330 g; the pulp is sweetened.
- **Numbers:** about 205 ml, 9.1% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks, 14.3:1. Golden amber.
- **Why:** the char's caramel meets the Medium's toasted caramel; the pinch of salt follows
  mango's savoury family in Cobber (NPMI +0.10, above chance). Mango and lime is well trodden
  (10 bar drinks).

### Mango y Manzanilla

- **Per serve:** 97.5 ml from the fridge into a chilled glass.
- **Batch (7 serves, 700 ml rail):** 315 ml mango pisco, 140 ml La Guita Manzanilla, 70 ml dry vermouth,
  17.5 ml simple syrup, 6 ml Angostura, 1 ml 20% saline, 140 ml water. About 22% ABV, 1.7 standard
  drinks, dry. Use within 2 weeks.
- **Mango pisco:** 300 g diced ripe mango in 700 ml pisco, fridge, 48 hours, then strain through
  a fine sieve and a coffee filter. Pale gold.
- **Why:** savoury is mango's strongest usable family in Cobber; mango has bar-drink support with
  dry vermouth (White Lies, Mango y Machado) and Angostura (Mango No. 3).

**Mango and nut allergies:** mango is in the cashew family, like the pink peppercorn the bar
ruled out. Cross-reactions with cashew or pistachio are reported but inconsistent, and mango flesh
is usually tolerated, so it isn't a nut, but staff should know if a guest with a cashew or
pistachio allergy asks.

**Seasons:** strawberries are at their best in Sydney through spring; Kensington Pride mangoes run
November to March.

## Mon Chéri: the spec to test (decided 2026-10-22)

Everything else in this file is background; this is the one to make.

**Shrub (overnight maceration, about 1.1 L, 37 drinks), changed from the blitz 2026-10-22 because
it keeps better without sorbate:**
1. The day before: 1 kg frozen strawberries, 400 g white sugar and 200 g demerara, covered, in
   the cool room for 24 hours. Stir once if you pass.
2. Strain, pressing lightly: about 1 L syrup. Bin the pulp.
3. Whisk in 120 ml FORVM Chardonnay vinegar, 21 g citric, 7 g malic and 1.5 g salt.
4. Taste 15 ml with 45 ml cold soda. If it's flat, add 3 g citric and 1 g malic, taste again; at
   most twice.
5. Bottle, fridge, rest 2 days. Shake before each round.
6. **Keeping, without sorbate:** about 3–4 weeks in the fridge (5 °C or below; not yet tested).
   Freeze half on day 0 if it sells slowly. Throw it out early if it fizzes or hisses when
   opened, grows a film, or smells yeasty or boozy. With 2 ml sorbate stock, a month or more.
   It keeps better than the blitz (about 2 weeks) because it's sweeter (about 0.57 g sugar per
   ml against 0.47), has more vinegar (11% against 9%) and has no pulp.

**Batch (700 ml rail, 12 drinks):** 510 ml pisco, 190 ml Elegante Medium.

**Build:** highball full of cubes, 55 ml batch, 30 ml shrub, top with 90 ml soda, one lift.
Garnish: 2–3 freeze-dried strawberry slices. About 185 ml, 10% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks.

Later tests, one at a time, only after this works: hojicha as the hot liquid, La Guita instead of
the Medium, black pepper instead of the garnish.

## Strawberry highballs: one prep day

Refined 2026-10-21; Fresa Tostada (the hybrid) is the spring/summer pick. Two strawberry highballs from one bag of frozen strawberries and one overnight
maceration: the raw syrup becomes the shrub, and the leftover fruit is caramelised in a pan. Both
are built over ice from the pisco–Medium rail batch (40:15, 55 ml a drink, shared with Mango
Asado), with nothing fresh at service: the acid is in the preps.

The Shortcake (brown-butter pisco) is dropped. Fresa Entera's raw-and-roasted idea and Fresa
Miso's caramel are now one drink, Fresa Caramelo: the caramel comes from the pan, not from a
separate sugar caramel, and there's no miso.

### The prep

**The day before (5 minutes):** 1 kg frozen strawberries (they come hulled; no need to thaw) and
600 g sugar in a covered tub in the cool room, 12–24 hours.

**Prep day (about 30 minutes):**
1. **Strain** through a sieve over a jug, without pressing: about 950 ml raw syrup and 350 g
   soft fruit.
2. **Shrub:** whisk 600 ml of the raw syrup with 150 ml sherry vinegar (Vinagre de Jerez, 7%
   acidity) and 15 g citric acid until the citric dissolves. About 755 ml, 25 drinks.
3. **Pan caramel:** the strained fruit and 100 g sugar in a wide pan (about 28 cm) over
   medium-high heat, in one layer. Leave it 3–4 minutes while the juice boils off, then scrape
   every 30 seconds until the syrup is amber and the edges catch: 6–8 minutes in all. Stop at
   amber; dark is bitter. The fruit is already soft from freezing, so this caramelises the sugar
   and juice rather than stewing the fruit.
4. **Deglaze:** off the heat, add 250 ml water (stand back: it spits), scrape up everything stuck
   to the pan (that's the caramel), simmer 1 minute, then strain, pressing hard. About 350 ml.
5. **Caramel cordial:** the pan liquid, the remaining 350 ml raw syrup, 17 g citric acid, 4 g
   malic acid and 1 g salt. About 700 ml, 23 drinks.
6. Bottle, label and date both. A month in the fridge (sugar and acid; 2 ml sorbate stock in each
   for insurance); freeze half if they sell slowly.

### Fresa y Jerez (the bright one)

- **Per drink:** cubes in a highball, 55 ml pisco–Medium batch, 30 ml shrub, top with 90 ml soda,
  one lift with a bar spoon.
- **Numbers:** about 185 ml, 10.1% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks, 13.3:1. Rosy coral.
- **Why:** strawberries with vinegar is the balsamic trick, here with vinegar made from sherry,
  so the sherry shows up twice. Strawberry's strongest flavour family in Cobber's recipe corpus
  is roasted-nutty (NPMI +0.46, above chance), hence the Medium.

### Making the shrub version (Mon Chéri) better: things to test

In order of payoff for the effort:

1. **Rest the shrub 2–3 days before using it.** Shrubs mellow as they sit: the vinegar's sharp edge
   softens and folds into the fruit. Same prep, just made earlier; it keeps a month.
2. **Season it: 1.5 g salt per batch.** Not enough to taste salty; it lifts the fruit and the
   sweetness, the same way salt works in a fruit salad.
3. **Swap some citric for malic.** Strawberry's own acids are mostly citric with some malic, and
   malic gives a rounder, longer sourness. Use about 3 parts citric to 1 part malic (amounts
   below).
4. **Use FORVM Chardonnay for all of the shrub's vinegar** (it's raw, so its flavour survives)
   if real sherry vinegar isn't available. It's milder, so raise the acids as below.
5. **Cold, full, gentle.** Batch, shrub and soda straight from the fridge, the glass filled to
   the top with cubes (less melt), soda poured down a bar spoon, one lift only. Most flat
   highballs are warm or over-stirred. Use bottled soda if the gun runs weak.
6. **Protect the pink.** Strawberry's red pigment is happiest in acid, cold and dark: keep the
   shrub in the fridge in a dark or covered bottle, and out of the spirit batch. If it dulls by
   week 3, 1–2 ml hibiscus colour drops per batch bring it back.
7. **Test the sherry:** Elegante Medium (toasty, the current pick) against La Guita Manzanilla
   (salty, brighter) in the batch. If the Medium wins but hides, take it to 20 ml.
8. **Optional:** a grind of black pepper over the top (strawberry's classic partner), tasted
   against the freeze-dried strawberry garnish. One or the other, not both.

**QC:** taste each new batch of shrub 1:3 with cold soda before service. It should taste like a
sour strawberry soda: if it's flat, add citric 2 g at a time; if it's sharp, give it another day.

**Shrub-only batch** (if the shrub wins, all the syrup goes into it):
- Day before: 1 kg frozen strawberries and 600 g sugar, covered, cool room.
- Strain without pressing (about 950 ml syrup). Press the leftover fruit gently for another
  100 ml or so if you don't mind a hazier shrub.
- Whisk in the vinegar, 1.5 g salt and the acids (amounts from the bump test below), then 2 ml
  sorbate stock. About 1.2 L, 40 drinks.
- **Vinegar and acids:** set by the bump test below. Neat FORVM tasted sharp (2026-10-22), as any
  vinegar does on its own. 240 ml FORVM per 950 ml syrup, then 22 g citric and 6 g malic, is the
  ceiling, not the start.

**Blitz method (the old bar's way; same day, no overnight wait):**
1. Make 250 ml hot liquid (water, or the tea below) and pour it over 750 g sugar and 1 kg frozen
   strawberries in the blender.
2. Blitz until smooth and the sugar has dissolved: rub a drop between your fingers, no grit. The
   frozen berries keep it cold, so nothing cooks.
3. Fine-sieve, pressing lightly; don't force the pulp through. About 1.45 L.
4. Whisk in the salt, then the vinegar and acid from the bump test, and 2 ml sorbate stock.
   Bottle, rest, shake before each round: whole-fruit shrub settles.

Against the overnight maceration: done in one session, nothing thrown away, more strawberry and
more body (it reads "juicy"), opaque pink rather than clear. The blender beats air in, which
browns strawberry faster, so add the vinegar and acid as soon as it's sieved, and let any foam
settle overnight before bottling. The whole fruit and the hot liquid dilute it, so pour 35 ml a
drink instead of 30 and top with 85 ml soda (taste).

**What else can go in the hot liquid** (pick one at most; the drink is already pisco, sherry,
strawberry):
- **Hojicha** (pick): Japanese green tea roasted over charcoal: toasty, caramel and nutty, with
  little bitterness. 15 g in 250 ml water at 90–95 °C for 5 minutes, strained; make it strong,
  it's seasoning. It brings the toasted note that meets the Elegante Medium, with no pan caramel,
  and it fits strawberry's strongest flavour family in Cobber (roasted-nutty). Japanese grocers
  or tea shops.
- **Demerara for 250 g of the sugar:** caramel without an extra ingredient. It browns the pink
  towards coral.
- **Hibiscus:** a small amount for colour only (the colour drops, 5–10 ml per batch). Steeped as
  the whole liquid, it's a fourth flavour, and it's the old bar's blackberry–hibiscus shrub.
- **Black pepper:** 10 grinds in the blender, sieved out with the seeds.

Not apple cider vinegar, even though the old bar used it: apple is an owner overlap. FORVM, set
by the bump test, does the same job.

**Bump test: vinegar first, then acid.** The vinegar brings the character (and the prickle); the
citric and malic bring clean brightness. So find the vinegar first, then top up the sourness.

1. **Acid stock:** 22.5 g citric and 7.5 g malic (3:1, strawberry's own balance), dissolved in
   warm water and topped up to 100 ml. That's 0.3 g acid per ml, the same strength as the cobbler
   acid solutions. Label it; it keeps for months.
2. **Start:** 200 ml strawberry syrup and a pinch of salt, no vinegar, no acid. Every taste is
   15 ml of it with 45 ml cold soda over ice (the 1:3 QC mix); sip and spit. A 5 ml syringe from
   the pharmacy makes the measuring exact.
3. **Vinegar bumps:** add 10 ml FORVM (5 ml per 100 ml syrup), stir, taste. Repeat up to 5 times
   (25 ml per 100 ml, the old amount). Stop at the last bump where you get the vinegar's winey,
   oaky depth with no prickle in the nose or throat. It will still taste sweet and soft here;
   that's fine. Expect to land around 10–15 ml per 100 ml, half the old amount.
4. **Acid bumps:** on that, add 2 ml acid stock (1 ml per 100 ml, 0.3 g acid), stir, taste.
   Repeat up to 8 times. Note the first bump that tastes like bright strawberry and the one where
   it tips into sharp; take the one before sharp if in doubt.
5. **Confirm blind** in two half-size finished drinks (27.5 ml batch, 15 ml shrub, 45 ml soda):
   your pick against one acid bump lower.
6. **Scale:** per 100 ml syrup, your vinegar bumps × 5 ml and your acid bumps × 1 ml stock. For
   example, 2 vinegar bumps and 4 acid bumps on 950 ml syrup is 95 ml FORVM and 38 ml stock (or
   8.6 g citric and 2.9 g malic weighed dry). The vinegar mellows a little more over 2 days of
   rest, so a fresh pick that's right will soften slightly in service.

What right tastes like: strawberry first, a clean sourness at the front, a little lingering tang,
and no prickle. If the prickle arrives before the depth does, the vinegar is the limit: stay
lower on it and let the acid stock do the sourness.

### Fresa Caramelo (the deep one)

- **Per drink:** the same build with 30 ml caramel cordial.
- **Numbers:** about 185 ml, 10.1% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks, 12.8:1. Deeper red.
- **Why:** furaneol, strawberry's signature molecule, smells of both caramel and strawberry, so
  caramelising the fruit's own sugar pushes the strawberry further, while the raw syrup in the
  cordial keeps it bright. The Medium's toasted caramel meets the pan caramel.
- **One bottle to buy:** Elegante Medium ($25, $19.99 in a six). Its own notes are nuts, cocoa and
  a toasted-caramel finish; it's already oxidised, so it doesn't fade once open the way a fino
  does; and it's the same bottle as La Pasión's pick. At 15 ml a drink, one bottle is 50 drinks.
  It does pull the colour from red towards brick.
- **If it tastes heavy:** deglaze the pan with 50 ml sherry vinegar plus 200 ml water instead of
  250 ml water (a gastrique: caramel deglazed with vinegar), and cut the citric to about 13 g.
  That gives it the shrub's brightness without a second prep.
- **Test against:** the same cordial with Tío Pepe Fino in the batch instead of the Medium
  (Fresa Entera's pairing): toasted almond and bread, more strawberry tart than caramel.

### Fresa Tostada (the hybrid: the spring/summer pick)

Mostly the shrub's fresh strawberry, with the caramel underneath. The pan caramel is deglazed with
the sherry vinegar (a gastrique) and mixed back into all the raw syrup, so it's one cordial, not
two.

- **Prep (one maceration, about 35 minutes on the day):**
  1. The day before: 1 kg frozen strawberries and 500 g white sugar, covered, cool room.
  2. Strain without pressing: about 900 ml raw syrup and 350 g soft fruit.
  3. Pan-caramelise the fruit with 100 g demerara to deep amber (6–8 minutes). Demerara gets there
     faster and its sotolon is the caramel-maple note of the aged sherry (Cobber's demerara
     profile is sotolon).
  4. Off the heat, deglaze with 100 ml sherry vinegar and 100 ml water (open the window: hot
     vinegar stings), scrape, simmer 1 minute, strain pressing hard.
  5. Combine with all the raw syrup, then whisk in the other 100 ml sherry vinegar cold, 20 g
     citric, 5 g malic and 1 g salt. Half the vinegar is cooked (mellow, part of the caramel) and
     half raw (bright). About 1.2 L, 40 drinks; a month in the fridge (2 ml sorbate stock for
     insurance).
- **Vinegar if there's no sherry vinegar** (supermarket shelves, 2026-10-22): deglaze with a cheap
  white wine vinegar (Moro or La Nova, about $3.50 for 500 ml) and use FORVM Chardonnay vinegar
  (Penedès, oak-aged by a solera-like method, 70% Chardonnay vinegar and 30% grape juice, about
  $23–26 for 250 ml) for the raw half. Cook with the cheap one, finish with the good one: one
  FORVM bottle covers 2½ batches, about 25c a drink. It's milder than sherry vinegar, so raise
  the citric to about 23 g. Skip the Cabernet FORVM (red, it browns the pink), apple cider
  vinegars (apple is an owner overlap), the raspberry, pomegranate, date and fig-honey-vanilla
  caramelised balsamics (overlaps), dark balsamic and red wine vinegar (colour), and glazes.
  Caramelised white balsamic (for example Tar10) is mostly sugar and burnt sugar with a little
  vinegar: it could stand in for the pan step, but it's generic caramel, not toasted strawberry.
- **Per drink:** the same build: cubes, 55 ml pisco–Medium batch, 30 ml cordial, 90 ml soda, one
  lift.
- **Numbers:** about 185 ml, 10.1% ABV, 1.5 standard drinks, 13.6:1, sugar about 8.8 g per 100 ml.
  The caramel is about a quarter of the cordial.
- **More caramel, to meet the Medium:** take the pan to the dark side of amber, or raise the Medium
  to 20 ml (pisco stays twice the sherry). **Fresher:** caramelise only half the strained fruit
  and stir the rest straight into the syrup before straining again.

What to expect (not yet tasted): fresh strawberry and pisco's grape first, a toasted-caramel
middle where the Medium joins in, then a bright, vinegar-sharpened finish that keeps it
thirst-quenching. Strawberries with balsamic under a crème-brûlée crust, in a highball.

### Garnish

- **Freeze-dried strawberry slices** (pick): 2–3 on top of the ice. Shelf-stable for months in an
  airtight jar, pink, and they smell of strawberry as they soften, with no fresh fruit behind the
  bar. From bulk-food or health-food shops.
- **Basil:** it's a herb, so it breaks the house rule (no herby flavours) unless the owner signs
  off, and fresh basil blackens in days, the same problem as fresh strawberries.
- **Black pepper** (Fresa y Jerez only): one grind over the top is strawberry's classic partner.
  Cobber has no data on it, and it's an extra step at service.
